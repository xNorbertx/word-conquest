What the numbers suggest.

A weaker word for better land

Chosen moves with ≥2 fewer word points and ≥3 more territory swing than the best-word-score alternative. Same 64 seeds, 128 balanced games each.

Higher land values: +7.9 percentage points; seed-bootstrap 95% interval +6.6 to +9.2. This measures a bot's choices, not whether they are always good.

Holding can overwhelm words

Income as a share of final scores in the larger limited-attention comparison, all playing styles.

Long-word solvers finish in fewer rounds and earn less income. A recurring reward therefore changes balance with word-finding skill.

Strategy already exists. In the small reply-aware check, planning ahead beat word-only play in 20 of 24 games under current rules. The task is making those decisions more frequent and clearer—not replacing word finding with automatic points.

How the study was run—and what it cannot tell us

The pinned game engine validates every chosen word, scores it and replaces letters. An isolated adapter supplies experimental holding and board rules. Tests check engine agreement, legal search paths, mirroring, round timing, caps and one-time rewards.

Familiar words use wordfreq 3.1.1, intersected with the actual game dictionary at Zipf frequency ≥3.5. Most searches consider words up to nine letters. The limited-attention model samples eight familiar word types, favoring shorter and more frequent words. It does not measure human vocabulary or attention.

Word-first, territory-first, balanced, points-per-letter and a small sampled-reply planner were tested. Mirror pairs swap the opening letters between sides. Rule comparisons reuse seed sets; altered layouts can consume random numbers differently. New seeds were used after screening.

Holding is assessed after both players move. Tiles must have remained owned since the previous round boundary. Fresh captures cannot earn a reward in the capture round. Maturity is lost on capture.

Human enjoyment, time to find a word, fatigue and multi-day engagement remain unmeasured. The solver rarely needed to refresh, which proves words exist, not that a person will easily see them. First-player balance and endgame steals remain playtest concerns.

Suggested human playtest

Start with 6–10 paired matches: current rules versus land 2 / castles 5. Alternate who starts. Keep the board and jokers unchanged. Record the moments when you sacrifice word points, whether the move survives a reply, whether castles feel mandatory, and whether the final player steals an unfair win.

Then test 45 tiles / four jokers separately. If defense still feels missing, try mature territory before recurring income.
