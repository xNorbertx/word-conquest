What actually changes?

Seed 50000: the first held-out board, not a hand-picked success. Ochre outlines mark the perimeter. Bold green letters have moved within their original region.

01

Draw the usual supply.

Keep the language-specific draw weights and category budgets. Each starting region retains its existing vowel and consonant allocation.

02

Try better arrangements.

Swap two letters only within the same category and region. A vowel stays a vowel. Every individual letter count stays exactly the same.

03

Help the edges and the centre.

Reward useful, distinct neighbouring letters everywhere, with extra priority where there are fewer than eight neighbours. Stop after at most three improvement passes.

After a turn or refresh, rearrange only newly replaced letters. Untouched tiles keep their letters. A swap cannot restore a tile’s previous letter.

Choose a letter to see its five strongest partners. Pairs work in either direction: a word can travel either way across the board.

Strength is a normalized pairing measure, not a probability or draw percentage. Examples are accepted words from the research subset. Duplicate neighbours count once, favouring a useful mix over a cluster of identical letters.

The calculation
Pair strength(A, B) = words with adjacent AB or BA / sqrt(words containing A * words containing B)
Tile support = sum of the three strongest DISTINCT neighbouring letters
Tile priority = 1 + (8 - number of neighbours) / 2
Arrangement quality = sum of tile support * tile priority

A joker supplies full support. Swaps must increase total quality.
Categories never change position. Starting halves and bands keep their letter pools.

Each qualifying word counts once. Very frequent words do not overwhelm the profile. Letter counts, pair strengths and alphabet handling are calculated independently for each language.

Opening boards

Distinct accepted words of 3–7 letters, at most one joker, no reused tiles. Edge options start on each ordinary perimeter tile, considered as a possible owned starting point. Whole-board options allow any start; actual opening moves start on owned tiles.

A stricter vocabulary check

72 complete simulated games

12 seed pairs per language. The same simple policy chooses words by points plus a small castle/capture preference. Both versions play to the finish with the real server engine, dictionary checks, replacement letters and scoring. Different choices lead to different positions: this is a stress test, not a controlled measure of human speed.

Alternatives tested on the tuning boards

48 matched seeds per language (2000–2047), eight candidates plus the baseline. The chosen method preserves each tile’s category, favours varied neighbours and rewards the centre too. Larger edge-only gains sometimes cost options elsewhere.

What this cannot tell us

  • More legal options do not guarantee that people spot words faster. No human timing study was run.
  • Longer words, multiple-joker words and uncommon vocabulary remain legal, but are outside this study’s search scope.
  • Some individual boards get worse. An average gain is not a guarantee for every seed or player.
  • Equal starting letter budgets do not guarantee equal word options. Starting player selection remains random.
  • The app’s new outline only identifies legal next tiles. It does not suggest words or check dictionary validity.

Where the evidence comes from

The acceptance filter is the pinned game dictionary: Letterpress for English; Martin Lindhe’s list plus SALDO morphology for Swedish; OpenTaal for Dutch. This change does not expand those dictionaries. SAOL is not included.

wordfreq 3.1.1 by Robyn Speer supplies the familiarity proxy. It combines several text sources and is a historical snapshot, not a live language service. The filter is Zipf ≥ 3.5 (roughly three occurrences per million), 3–7 letters, alphabetic forms, intersected with the legal dictionary. Accents are folded conservatively; Swedish Å, Ä and Ö stay separate. Dutch IJ stays two tiles.

Validation uses unseen board seeds 50000–50255. The stricter check uses Zipf ≥ 4.0 and 64 other pairs (70000–70063). Full games use seeds 80000–80011. Optimized production code matched the researched algorithm on all 768 held-out boards.
