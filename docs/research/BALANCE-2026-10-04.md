# Word Conquest balance study — 4 October 2026

**Recommendation: test higher territory values first; test a smaller board separately. If holding matters, prefer territory that matures in value over income from every tile.** Keep the current live rules until human games confirm the trade-offs.

I ran **8,736 complete self-play games across 33 setups**, covering **215,748 turns**, plus **640 counterfactual continuations** of five saved decisions. The original 0.4.0 client, server, rule snapshots and player data were not changed. The study uses the actual pinned engine and dictionary, with explicitly isolated adapters for new mechanics.

Open [the interactive report](balance-2026-10-04.html) for all ratings, comparison tables and eight complete game replays. The [result data](balance-2026-10-04.json) and [reproduction instructions](balance/README.md) are included. Large per-game logs and the vocabulary cache stay in ignored `work/balance/`.

## What the game already does well

Territory already has a strategic role: it changes the score difference, provides new starting points and can deny useful starting points to an opponent. A small reply-aware bot check won **20/24 games against a word-only bot under current rules**; the corresponding figures were 18/24 with higher territory values and 19/24 with castle income or mature territory. Those small samples do not rank the variants reliably, but they counter the idea that strategy is absent from today's game.

The problem is making those decisions frequent, legible and enjoyable for people. Automated agents cannot measure that last part.

## Recommended experiments

The /5 ratings below are **my design judgments about fit to your stated goal**, not measured fun scores or statistically calibrated rankings. I considered the observed trade-offs, preservation of satisfying words, interaction, potential runaway leads and the burden of explaining a rule. No variant earns 5/5 without human playtesting.

| Setup | Estimated fit | Why test it / main reservation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary land **2**, castles **5**; otherwise current rules | **4/5** | Clearest increase in word-versus-territory choices. Watch last-turn steals and second-player advantage. |
| **45 tiles, four jokers**, unchanged scoring | **4/5** | Contact happens much sooner; lower joker density than six on that small board. May feel more confrontational and less spacious. |
| Territory matures after a full held round: ordinary **1 → 2**, castle **3 → 5** | **3.5/5** | Rewards defending without accumulating an income stream. Needs a clear visual distinction for mature tiles. |
| Current 69-tile / six-joker rules | **3.5/5** | Already supports tactics and good words. A useful control, not a failed design. |
| Held castles **+1 per round, capped at +2** | **3/5** | Restrained recurring reward, but adds bookkeeping for a modest measured change in choices. |
| Castle held for a round pays **3 once per player per castle** | **3/5** | Bounded reward avoids compounding; effect was modest. |
| Four jokers on the current 69-tile board | **2.5/5** | Reduces wildcard chains, but slows conflict and increases moves entirely inside owned land. |
| Held castles **+2 each every round** | **2.5/5** | Meaningful incentives, but about a fifth of the score becomes income; comeback results were less encouraging. |
| Every held tile **+1 each round** | **1.5/5** | Income dominates the word game. Not the balance requested. |

Do not combine the first three immediately. A scoring-only change gives a cleaner human comparison; a smaller board is a separate, stronger change to the feel of the game.

## What changed in the larger comparison

This table uses **128 balanced-versus-balanced games per setup**, on the same 64 new seeds and their mirrored boards. The bot considers eight sampled familiar word types per turn and chooses the most useful available path. The full study also includes mixed playing styles and stronger word search.

A **territorial word sacrifice** means choosing at least 2 fewer word points than the highest-word-score available option, while getting at least 3 more points of territory swing. Swing includes your territory gain plus the opponent's loss. This metric reflects choices made by the specified bot; a higher rate is not proof that those choices win more games.

| Rules | Territorial word sacrifices | Moves that capture an enemy tile | Income share of final scores | First-player win share, draws halved |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Current | 12.2% | 45.9% | 0% | 47.3% |
| Land 2 / castles 5 | 20.1% | 51.1% | 0% | 39.1% |
| Mature territory | 15.8% | 48.8% | 0% | 44.5% |
| Castles earn 2 per held round | 12.4% | 45.0% | 21.6% | 46.9% |

The paired increase in territorial sacrifices was **+7.9 percentage points** for higher land values (seed-cluster bootstrap 95% interval **+6.6 to +9.2**) and **+3.6 points** for mature territory (**+2.3 to +4.8**). These intervals describe sampling variation in these bot models, not uncertainty about human enjoyment. Exploratory variants were selected after screening; the larger comparisons used new seeds. The intervals are not adjusted for multiple comparisons.

The lower first-player result with stronger land values is a real warning to investigate, not a solved fairness issue. Raising territory values also raises the value of the final reply. Do not compensate the first player automatically from this study alone.

## Holding territory: avoid turning it into a rent game

A held-tile reward generated roughly **64% of final points** across the larger limited-attention mixed-style comparison. In the exhaustive familiar-word comparison it contributed much less, because long words finished games in fewer rounds. That makes the balance depend heavily on how long people's words are.

Capping all-territory income at 4 per round often made both sides reach the cap early. It changed the score totals more than the choices. The deliberate short-word/points-per-letter bot did **not** demonstrate a dominant income exploit: balanced play still won about 54% of the all-tile-income matches. The objection is the dominant scoring share and compounding incentive, not a proven unbeatable exploit.

The mature-territory prototype has no recurring payment: a fresh normal tile is worth 1, then 2 after surviving a complete round; a castle goes from 3 to 5. Capturing it resets its maturity. Banked word points remain untouched. A cheaper alternative is the one-time castle holding reward. Both are worth human testing before recurring income.

For these prototypes a round means one move by each player. Maturity/rewards are checked after player 2's move, and require continuous ownership from the previous round boundary. Newly captured tiles cannot pay in the capture round. This timing, including the final round, needs explicit explanation and may itself affect seat balance.

## Jokers and the map

Six permanent jokers are powerful. With exhaustive familiar-word search, around 69% of chosen words in the initial current-rules screen used more than one joker, and average word length was 8.5. That is far above the roughly **5.4 letters** in the 19 words you supplied; the solver is not a typical player. The limited-attention model averages around 4.5 letters and is also only a proxy.

Simply reducing jokers on the large board was not a clear improvement. It reduced multi-joker use but also delayed contact and reduced attacks. Clustering jokers centrally had the opposite problem: routes through the same wildcard cluster dominated. Eight jokers made this stronger. Moving four neutral jokers to spaced bridge positions helped contact modestly, but did not solve multi-joker dependence.

The more promising combination was **45 tiles with four jokers**, retaining one home joker each and two neutral jokers. In a separate fresh-board comparison (64 balanced games per variant):

- First enemy capture: **move 5.8 instead of 10.5**.
- Moves with an enemy capture: **66.1% instead of 38.4%**.
- First-player win share: **48.4% in both**; this does not prove fairness.
- Normalized final margin: **12.2% versus 12.3%**.

The paired contact improvement was 4.7 moves (95% seed-bootstrap interval 3.1–6.2). This is a joint board-size/joker-count experiment; it does not establish that either change alone causes the improvement. Six jokers on the small board produced much heavier wildcard use.

I also tested one- and two-joker-per-word limits, consumable jokers, a higher vowel target, a longer letter budget, fixed rounds, central castles, an endgame castle-majority bonus, gentler long-word bonuses and refreshing only captured letters. None displaced the recommendations above. All 33 configurations and judgments are in the interactive report.

## Concrete decisions and failed intuitions

In one saved higher-land/four-joker endgame, **SONG** offered 5 word points and an 8-point territory swing, winning by 2. **PEACE** offered 9 word points and no territory swing, losing by 2. The final reply was already guaranteed, so this is an exact comparison rather than a speculative rollout. The interactive replay opens just before that decision. A second example under higher land values alone was RULE (4 word points, 18 territory swing, win by 1) versus RUTH (6 word points, 10 territory swing, lose by 5).

Midgame attacks are less certain. I took the first qualifying early/midgame sacrifices from saved seed-1000 replays, without choosing them based on their outcomes, and ran 64 sampled continuations for each alternative. Four of the five territorial choices had a lower estimated final margin; the fifth was essentially tied. Some differences were uncertain. The SITS-versus-SELECT castle attack, for example, gave up too much in expectation. This is why the report does not equate “more captures” with “better play.”

## Limits and next human test

These bots have neither human vocabulary nor human patience. The familiarity proxy is [wordfreq 3.1.1](https://github.com/rspeer/wordfreq), intersected with the actual game dictionary. Corpus frequency does not establish whether a person knows or likes a word. The word-only and land-first bots are deliberately simple; the reply-aware check is small and samples only two hypothetical replacement/reply outcomes per candidate. No policy is claimed to be optimal.

Most searches stop at nine letters; checks at twelve letters and with the complete dictionary broadly preserve the stronger-land signal. There were no search-node-limit cutoffs in the recorded study. Legal word availability was high and refreshes almost absent, which only shows that the solvers can find words—not that people will find them easily. Human frustration, accessibility, session timing and enjoyment remain unmeasured.

Next: play **6–10 paired matches of current scoring versus land 2 / castles 5**, alternating who starts. Keep the board and jokers unchanged. Note when you deliberately give up word points, whether the choice feels understandable, whether it remains worthwhile after the reply, whether castles become mandatory, and whether the last mover can steal an unfair win. Then test the 45-tile/four-joker board separately. If holding is still missing, try mature territory before income.

No new paid infrastructure, live deployment, account access or player-data changes were needed for this study.
